DocketStamp

A guided path from self-help letter to signed, mailed document.

DocketStamp turns a multi-step administrative letter task into a guided product: deterministic templates, a consistent preview, human review and a clear mailing handoff.

Product leadership · Production software delivery

Back to selected ventures Read the SendMyNotes case study →

01 · The problem

Turn a multi-step letter task into a clear sequence.

A personal letter can start with a clear intent and still become a tangle of details, formatting, signatures and postage. DocketStamp turns that process into a guided sequence, while the person sending it stays in control of the content and final signature.

02 · Product decisions

Build trust into each handoff.

  • Keep letter prose deterministic. The product fills templates from explicit user-provided details.
  • Put the person at the handoff. The user reviews the document and signs it before payment and any dispatch decision.
  • Freeze the reviewed version. A document snapshot is fixed before payment so the approved file is the one that enters the mailing workflow.
  • Check both views. The interactive preview and print rendering use the same interpolation logic, with template availability and geography treated as explicit product rules.

03 · My role

Turn the product promise into a working system.

I lead product direction, experience and software delivery. I turn the service promise into clear decisions about templates, review, print behavior, payment and postal handoff—then coordinate focused agent work and take responsibility for integration and release.

04 · Delivery approach

A delivery system with clear ownership and release gates.

I organize agent work into focused lanes—backend behavior, interface, assets and independent critique—with shared contracts between them. I own product choices, integrate each lane and verify the end-to-end user and hosted experience before release.

  1. Parallel work, one contract: template availability work moved across backend and interface lanes, then came together against a shared contract and browser review.
  2. Close the release gap: a staging check surfaced missing public images on share previews. We repaired the packaging path, then verified the assets and rendered pages in staging and production.
  3. Keep people in control: the letter content is deterministic, and the person sending it reviews and signs before mailing. Agents help me develop and inspect the software.

05 · Production release

Ship the workflow, not just the interface.

I took DocketStamp from product concept through production software releases. That work includes template-availability controls and public share previews, with checks for page metadata, hosted assets and rendered pages. The release path brings product rules and the user-facing experience together.

06 · Product leadership lesson

Trust is built at the handoffs.

A strong experience makes each transition clear: information into a document, review into approval, approval into fulfillment and software changes into release. The same product discipline guides how I lead teams and build with agents.
